00 — the 30-second version

What throughline is now

You keep a vault of the things that make you you — films, music, books, quotes you live by, plus your own notes on what you're chasing and what you're scared of. throughline reads all of it and becomes the brain on top: it knows your taste, it sees your patterns, and when you're stuck it pulls a thread and shows you a way forward.

Four things you'll do with it:

  • Know yourself, on paper. One living profile of your taste and your themes, drawn from your own vault.
  • Get unstuck. Tell it "I'm stuck on X." It asks a sharp question, connects something from your own past, and hands you one concrete next step. This is the heart.
  • Check if something's you. An image, a caption, a song for a reel — drop it in and see how well it fits who you are.
  • Find by feeling — in or out. Search your own vault by mood, or send your taste out into the web to discover new things you'd love.

Your vault lives in Obsidian and syncs through your own private git repo, so it's with you on any machine and owned entirely by you. The sensitive stuff never leaves your laptop.

01 — the two beliefs it's built on

Why it's shaped this way

Two rules sit under every feature. If a feature ever breaks one of them, the feature is wrong, not the rule.

1 — The app has no taste and no wisdom. You do.

A small local model isn't a genius art director or a life coach, and throughline never pretends otherwise. What it's good at is memory and connection: it remembers what you've decided matters, and it spots links across hundreds of your own notes faster than you can. You supply the taste and the judgment; it makes them repeatable — written down, searchable, connected. That's the honest version of "taste is the moat."

2 — It exists to get you moving, not to give you more to think about.

This is the load-bearing one. A "surface my patterns / show me insights about myself" tool is dangerously easy to build into a machine for over-thinking — and that's the last thing you need more of. So the brain is built to end on an action. Reflections and connections are allowed, but only ever as fuel for a next move. The default exit from any "stuck" moment is a single, concrete, doable thing — never a prettier mirror.

what these rules force

The app never edits your work for you and never overrides you — every claim it makes is editable and traceable. And the brain never leaves you holding pure analysis; if it can't point at a next step, it hasn't finished its job.

02 — the questions you walk up and ask

Four questions, one brain

Same as before — don't picture menus, picture one person you can ask things. There are now four kinds of questions, and the fourth is the one that matters most.

  • "What even am I?" → it reads your vault and writes your Profile.
  • "Does this fit me?" → you show it something, it runs a Fit Check.
  • "Find me something that feels like…" → a Vibe Search, inward (your vault) or outward (the web).
  • "I'm stuck — show me a way through." → the thread pulls connections and hands you a next step.
03 — how it's wired, in plain words

Your vault is the truth; throughline is the brain on top

The most important architecture decision: throughline doesn't own your data. Your Obsidian vault does. That means the app has to build far less — no storage, no clunky importer — and you keep everything in a form you can read and edit by hand forever.

everything is a note

Each thing you love, or each thing about you, is one note. A little header (the "frontmatter") tells the app what it is; the body is the human part — why it matters to you. That "why" is what the app actually learns from.

--- a movie you love --- type: movie title: Blade Runner 2049 feeling: loved tags: [nocturnal, lonely, neon] --- the rain, the silence, the loneliness of K. beautiful and alone at the same time — the exact thing i keep chasing in my own work. --- something about you --- type: ambition title: build a creative life i'm proud of tags: [bangkok, joji, freedom] --- i want work that's mine and a life with room to breathe. the move is the unlock. i keep planning it and not starting it.
two-way editing

You write notes in Obsidian (the nicer writing experience) or capture them quickly inside throughline — both write to the same files. Neither side is the "real" one; the vault is. throughline never holds a private copy you can't see.

sync & history = git

Your vault is a private git repo. That gives you three things for free: your data on any machine, a full version history of everything (git is the "version history" feature — no need to build one), and total ownership. The Obsidian Git plugin handles the commit/pull so you barely notice it.

three honest gotchas (so they don't bite later)
  • The app + the local model install per machine. Git syncs your data everywhere; the brain itself is a one-time setup on each laptop you use.
  • Big image piles get heavy in git. Use git-LFS for image attachments, or keep large image folders outside the vault and just point notes at them. Text notes weigh nothing.
  • The app's index doesn't go in git. The fingerprints/cache are big, machine-specific, and rebuildable — each machine builds its own from the vault. The vault is the truth; the index is just derived from it.

05
feature · the foundation

The Vault & Intake

The vault is your shelf of everything; the intake is the quiet worker that reads it. Together they replace the old "library + eyes pass" — now reading words as well as images.

what kinds of things live here
  • Taste: movies, songs, books, quotes — things you love, each with a line on why.
  • Self: ambitions, fears, experiences, plain journal notes — who you are and where you're headed.
  • Images: stills, references, your own frames — attachments in the vault.
  • You can add more types whenever; the app reads the type and adapts.
how things get in (both ways)
  1. In Obsidian: write a note however you like. throughline picks it up.
  2. In throughline — quick capture: a fast "add something" box. Paste a film title, a lyric, a thought; pick a type; it writes a clean note into the vault for you. For images, drag them in.
  3. Either way it lands as a real file you own, readable forever, in or out of the app.
what the intake records for each thing
  • For an image: a plain description, dominant colors (swatch + hex), light, mood words, framing.
  • For a note: the themes and feelings in it, and how it connects to your other notes.
  • For everything: a fingerprint — the hidden number-string that lets the app compare two things by feel. This is what makes search and connections work.
details that matter
  • Reading runs quietly in the background; a small counter shows progress. Big vaults don't freeze the app, and you can pause it if the laptop's warm.
  • It reads once and remembers — never re-reads on every open.
  • You can correct anything it tagged, and your correction sticks; the app never silently overwrites your edits.
  • If it can't read something confidently (a blurry image, a one-word note), it says so instead of inventing.

06
feature · know thyself

Your Profile

One unified picture of you, drawn from your vault: your taste and your themes, because the good connections only happen when it's all one fabric. The creative-taste part is still clearly its own section so you can read it alone.

what's in it
  • The creative slice (readable on its own): palette, mood, light signature, framing/shot grammar, recurring subjects, do's & don'ts.
  • The deeper slice: your recurring themes ("solitude as a thing you're drawn to, not just afraid of"), your stated ambitions, and the threads that keep reappearing across films, music, and your own notes.
  • A name you give it ("midnight joji").
how each claim earns its place
  1. Backed by examples. Every line says "because of these things" and links straight to them. No mystery pronouncements about who you are.
  2. Honest confidence. Each section says how much it's based on, so you know what to trust and what to feed.
  3. Written back to your vault as a note — so it's versioned by git and portable like everything else.
what you can do
  • Generate / regenerate it from the current vault. It drafts; you decide.
  • Edit any line in plain words, pin lines so regenerating won't touch them, add your own, delete what's wrong.
  • Roll back to an earlier version (it's just git history).
  • Export the creative slice as your portable joji brand book. export polish is later

07
feature · the heart

the thread

The reason the whole thing exists. When you're stuck, flat, or circling — you come here, say what's going on, and it pulls a thread: a sharp question, a connection from your own life you'd forgotten, and one concrete next step. It always ends on a move.

the core flow, step by step
  1. You type the stuck thing in plain words — "can't decide which reel to make next," "no motivation to start the thing," "feel flat about all of it."
  2. It asks one sharp question back first — not ten. The kind that actually unsticks your own head ("what's the version of this you'd be embarrassed to admit you want?").
  3. It surfaces a connection from your vault — something you already said, loved, or lived that's relevant. ("Three months ago you wrote almost this exact sentence. The thing that broke it then was starting small.")
  4. It hands you one next step — small, specific, doable today. Highlighted, unmissable. Not a plan, not five options. One move.
  5. You can ask for a different step, a smaller step, or say "did it" — and it remembers the momentum.
the ambient side (quiet, low-frequency)
  • Now and then — not constantly — it surfaces a thread it noticed: "you keep returning to X across your notes; want to pull on it?" You can open it or dismiss it.
  • No notification machine. No streaks, no nagging, no badges. It earns attention by being useful, not by interrupting. You set how often (default: rarely).
the rules that keep it healthy, not harmful
  • It always ends on an action. If it can't get to a concrete next step, it hasn't finished. Pure insight with no move is a failure state, by design.
  • It points at your own life, not platitudes. Every nudge is grounded in something you put in the vault — your taste, your past, your words.
  • It's a tool, not a friend. It won't pretend to be a relationship or a therapist, and it'll gently point you toward real people or real help when something's beyond a creative block. It's honest about being a small model with a good memory.
  • You can always close the loop. "Did it" is one tap, and that's the satisfying end — momentum logged, thread done.
why this shape, specifically for you

You think in circles when given options and move when given a question and a decision. So the thread is built to do exactly that: subtract choices, ask the right question, make one call. It's the opposite of a tool that hands you more to analyze.

08
feature · "is this me?"

Fit Check

A second pair of eyes on tap — now for more than images. Drop in a thumbnail, a caption draft, or a song you're eyeing for a reel, and get a straight read: how "you" is this, and exactly what's pulling it off — measured against your profile, never against generic "good."

what it works on
  • Images — a thumbnail, a frame, a reference. (palette / light / framing checks)
  • Words — a caption, a title, a line. ("does this sound like me?")
  • A song / track — by how its feel matches your taste. audio feel is later; words about it work now
what you get back
  • A fit score — one "how you is this" number, 0–100%, readable at a glance. (It's a distance measurement: how far this sits from the center of your taste.)
  • A one-line verdict — "strongly you, one thing to fix" / "drifts off."
  • A checklist tied to your profile — each profile line becomes a check, and each note names the rule it's checking. Never vague.
  • Concrete fixes — "warm the white balance," "this caption's too polished — you're looser than this."
  • Side-by-side — your thing next to the closest matches from your vault.
what you can do
  1. Check one thing deeply, or drop a batch to get them ranked by fit (pick the most-you option of ten).
  2. Disagree with any note — "I meant that" — and it stops flagging it and remembers your intent. This quietly tunes your profile over time.
  3. Send anything checked into the vault, or into the thread if it sparked something.
the hard line

It scores against you, never against generic taste, and it never edits the thing for you. Off-profile on purpose is a valid choice and it treats it as one — it'll tell you you've drifted, not that you're wrong.

feature · find by feeling

Vibe Search — two modes

One search bar, two directions. Look inward to find things already in your vault by feeling, or send your taste outward to discover new things you'd love. A clear toggle, and a clear wall between them.

inward — "what in my vault feels like this?"
  • A real ranking algorithm, not keyword matching. You type a feeling; it turns that into a fingerprint and ranks your vault by closeness of feel. "rainy, neon, alone" finds the right things even if nothing's named that.
  • Search by example too — drop an image or point at a note and get "more like this."
  • Explainable — each result shows why it surfaced ("matched on: warm light + solitary figure").
  • Tunable — weight it ("more by mood, less by color") and filter by type (films only, quotes only, my-work only).
  • Local, private, instant. This is the everyday mode.
outward — "what's out there that fits me?"
  • Takes your taste corpus and searches the live web for new films, albums, books you don't know yet that match it.
  • A recommender grounded in your actual taste instead of an engagement algorithm — the most portfolio-impressive part of the app.
  • Explainable — "suggested because it shares the nocturnal-loneliness thread you love."
  • One-tap to save a discovery straight into your vault as a new note.
  • outward is the v2 headliner; inward ships first
the wall, said plainly

Outward is the only place anything leaves your machine — your query plus a short summary of your taste. Your self material (fears, ambitions, journal) is never part of an outward query, ever. Inward never touches the web at all. You're always told which mode you're in.

10
the first ten minutes

The First Run

The first run's only job is to get you from "empty" to your first profile and your first thread — the two moments that make it click — as fast as possible.

  1. Point it at your vault — pick an existing Obsidian vault, or let it create one for you, wired to your private git repo. Privacy line up front: the sensitive stuff never leaves this machine.
  2. Seed it. If the vault's empty, a gentle prompt to add a first handful — a few films, a few songs, one honest note about what you're chasing. Quick capture makes this painless.
  3. Watch it read. A friendly progress moment in human terms ("reading your films, your notes, the feel of each…"), not a blank spinner.
  4. The reveal. Your first Profile. The "oh — that's me" moment, all backed by your own things.
  5. First thread. It gently offers one starter: "want to pull on a thread?" — so you feel the heart of the app on day one, ending on a tiny first move.

If you only added a few things, it still works — and it's honest: "thin first read; feed it more and it sharpens."

11
cross-cutting

Small but important rules

The vault is the truth — the app is disposable

Everything real lives in your vault as files you own. If throughline vanished tomorrow, your whole second brain would still be there, readable in plain Obsidian. The app is a brain you bolt on, never a cage your data lives in.

The privacy wall is sacred

Your self material — fears, ambitions, journal, experiences — is inward-only and never part of any web query. There's a clear, always-visible sign of which mode you're in. Privacy isn't a setting buried in a menu; it's the default and it's visible.

It points at action, always

The thread ends on a move. Fit Check ends on a fix. Search ends on a result you can use. Nothing in the app is allowed to leave you holding pure analysis — that's the one thing the whole product is designed against.

You correct it; it remembers

Every tag, profile line, fit note, and connection is editable, and your call sticks. It's a junior with a great memory: fast, tireless, sometimes wrong, never offended.

What the "fingerprint" actually is

When the app reads anything — image or note — it turns the feel into a long string of numbers. Similar feelings get similar numbers. That's the whole trick behind search, "more like this," and the connections the thread finds. You never see it; it just makes the magic parts work.

Honest about being small

Low-confidence reads, thin profile sections, empty searches, and moments beyond its depth — it says so plainly, and for anything heavier than a creative block it points you toward real people or real help. It would rather admit a gap than bluff.

Empty screens are invitations

Every screen tells you what to do to fill it. An empty thread says "what's on your mind?" An empty search says "try a feeling, like 'warm and lonely.'"

12
drawing the line

What's in v1 vs later

v1 has to deliver the heart — the thread — plus the minimum that makes it work: the vault, the profile, inward search. Fit Check rides along. The flashy outward discovery and the rest come right after, once you're opening this every day. The risk here isn't a small v1; it's a beautiful v-never. Ship the heart first.
